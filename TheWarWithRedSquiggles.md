The Beginning Stages of Using ESLint

I’ll be honest, the first day using ESLint was somewhat frustrating. At first, ESLint felt like an annoying teacher hovering over my shoulder, pointing out every mistake. Red squiggles popped up everywhere pointing to unused variables, inconsistent semicolons, and missing type annotations. Every time I thought I was done, a new error would pop up. I felt like I was spending more time fixing linting issues than solving the actual problem.

Some of the errors felt so small that I felt doing them was not needed, for example, warnings about extra spaces. Did those really matter? At first, I did not think so. However over time, I started to appreciate that having ESLint actually allowed me to focus on the logic of my program without having to worry about the inconsistencies. 

I also loved how ESLint told me exactly where the errors were. Instead of spending time searching through lines of codes to figure out why something was not compiling, I had instant feedback pointing to the problem. That saved me a lot of frustration and made me feel more in control of my work. 

New Habits

I also noticed my coding habits changing. I started to recognize the patterns of ESLint and what code it preferred and began spotting mistakes before ESLint did. For example, I really started to understand the difference between using const and using let. Although this took a lot of effort in the beginning, it helped me focus on solving problems instead of worrying about syntax. 

Another unexpected benefit of ESLint is that it has increased my confidence. Once I have cleared all the warnings and errors I feel reassured that my code is working properly. This means that if I have to go back to a specific project, I will not have to spend time questioning myself or certain lines of codes.

Why Coding Standards Matter

I realize coding standards are one of the most important things to learn in coding, especially when you have to work with others. They act as a common language for everyone on the team. Without these standards, even the best algorithm can get buried under messy, inconsistent code. 

Imagine working on a big group project and every team member writes code in a completely different style? That means the code would be harder to read, trace, and fix, taking a lot of time to just even get started. In other words, with coding standards, collaboration becomes easier and debugging takes less time.

Coding standards are like proper grammar in writing, you do not think about them consciously once you have  practiced enough, but they make your message much clearer to anyone reading it.

After one week using ESLint, I learned that coding standards are necessary in building good habits that shape the way I think about programming. They have already begun to improve the way I write code, and I know it is the start of building me into a better software engineer. 

Yes, some of the fixes felt unnecessary, like removing that one extra space, but now I see how important every little detail is in creating a clean, professional, and readable code. I again also love that I do not really have to waste time searching for errors because ESLint shows me exactly where to look. 

The process of using ESLint was not always easy, and sometimes it was irritating, but it was worth it. Coding standards may look like small details, but they are the foundation of building maintainable software.
