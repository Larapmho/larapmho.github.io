## I. Introduction
Artificial intelligence has become an increasingly common part of education, especially in software engineering, where students can use AI as support with debugging, understanding frameworks, and reinforcing concepts. In ICS 314, AI played a supportive role in my learning process because of the fast-paced nature of WODs, regular practice exercises, essays, and the final project. The only AI tool I consistently used was ChatGPT. I relied on it mainly as a tutor, a code explainer, and a debugging partner, making sure that the actual work I submitted reflected my own understanding and effort.

## II. Personal Experience with AI
When working through the Experience WODs, I occasionally used ChatGPT before the timer started to help me fully understand the instructions. This helped me make sure that I was fully prepared before I started the WOD. During the timed WODs themselves, I tried my best to avoid using AI because I wanted an accurate reflection of what I could do under pressure, but reviewing the concepts beforehand was extremely helpful. Nevertheless, I did have AI help me understand why my written code had issues and offer suggestions in fixing it. My usage of AI was similar in the in-class practice and timed WODs.

When writing the course essays, I sometimes used ChatGPT as a brainstorming assistant. I also used it to help me identify tools or resources that would deepen my understanding of the topic before I began writing. Once I felt more confident in the material, I wrote my own draft and then asked ChatGPT for help with grammar, clarity, and refining my explanations. Using AI in this way helped me organize my thoughts more effectively and improve the overall flow of my writing, making sure that readers could clearly understand what I was trying to communicate, whilst still making sure I was writing it on my own. 

The final project was where ChatGPT played a bigger role because the RoomMatch UHM website involved tools like Next.js, Prisma, Semantic UI React, and Playwright. For example, when Prisma gave me relational errors, I asked ChatGPT, “Why am I getting a missing relation field error in my schema?” or when ESLint kept complaining, I asked, “How do I resolve this ESLint warning in a Next.js project?” These explanations sped up my debugging process, but I always verified the suggestions with documentation. I also asked ChatGPT to explain unfamiliar tools by prompting things like, “Explain the difference between server components and client components in Next.js,” so I could approach the project with more confidence.

When learning new concepts and tutorials, ChatGPT was particularly helpful because I could ask clarifying questions repeatedly without feeling embarrassed. For example, I once typed, “Can you explain React props as if I am brand new?” and then followed up with, “Now explain it at a more advanced level.” This allowed me to build understanding gradually.

Furthermore, when I struggled to understand code in documentation, I pasted it into ChatGPT and wrote, “Explain this line by line.” This was incredibly useful when I was trying to understand Semantic UI React or more complex Playwright examples. It felt like having a tutor sit next to me, guiding me through each part without taking the learning out of my hands.

For quality assurance, ChatGPT became especially useful during debugging. When I saw vague error messages like “Cannot read properties of undefined,” I asked the AI to walk me through likely causes. It did not always give the exact answer, but it usually pointed me toward the right line or concept to inspect. This saved a lot of time and taught me how to think more like a debugger.

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding
Using ChatGPT genuinely improved my understanding of software engineering concepts because I could receive explanations instantly and ask follow-up questions without hesitation. It strengthened my confidence in concepts like React state, routing, Playwright testing, and Prisma relationships. At the same time, ICS 314 taught me the importance of not letting AI do too much for me. My real learning came from writing code myself, fixing my own mistakes, and practicing WODs without assistance. ChatGPT improved my comprehension, but the course structure ensured that I built real skills instead of relying on shortcuts.

## IV. Practical Applications Beyond ICS 314
Outside of ICS 314, I used ChatGPT in several real-world academic and personal projects. For example, in other ICS courses, it helped me understand algorithms or debug small coding issues. When working on my own projects or preparing applications, it helped me revise emails or explain unfamiliar technologies. These experiences showed me how AI can be a practical support tool in the broader software engineering world, especially when used carefully and critically.

## V. Challenges and Opportunities
The main challenge I experienced with ChatGPT was that it tends to simplify solutions if you are not extremely specific with the requirements. This became especially clear during the assignment where we had to take an AI-generated solution and test it. The code looked correct at first and even passed some of the simpler tests, but it ended up failing several of the edge-case tests because the AI had made assumptions that did not align with the exact requirements of the problem. That experience showed me how easy it is to trust an answer that “looks right” without fully understanding the logic underneath. 

Still, working through these mismatches taught me valuable problem-solving skills. Despite the challenges, the course showed me many opportunities as well. AI can help students learn faster, check their reasoning, structure their questions more clearly, and practice explaining difficult concepts. 

## VI. Comparative Analysis
Comparing traditional teaching methods with AI support, I found that both play important but different roles. The weekly modules, WODs, class exercises, and documentation taught me discipline, attention to detail, and the ability to solve problems without shortcuts. AI, on the other hand, provided immediate feedback, and alternative explanations when I was confused. Together, they create a balanced and modern learning experience.

## VII. Future Considerations
I believe AI will become a standard part of software engineering education in the future. As tools like ChatGPT continue to improve, they will likely become more tightly integrated into debugging tools, code editors, and learning platforms. The challenge will be making sure that students still learn the underlying principles and do not become overly dependent on AI. Courses like ICS 314 already demonstrate a healthy balance, showing students how to use AI responsibly without replacing foundational learning.

## VIII. Conclusion
Reflecting on my experience, ChatGPT played a meaningful role in my learning throughout ICS 314, helping me understand complex concepts, debug problems more efficiently, and prepare for WODs with greater confidence. At the same time, the course structure prevented me from becoming overly reliant on AI and pushed me to develop genuine coding skills. Moving forward, I believe AI should continue to be integrated into software engineering courses as a supportive tool, but always in a way that supports the student’s own mastery of the subject.
